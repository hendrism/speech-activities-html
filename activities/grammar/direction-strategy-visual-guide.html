Direction Strategy Chart

Use this visual to slow down, spot the most important parts of a direction, and choose the best strategy to ace it the first time.

1

Scan & Notice

Think: What is this asking me to do? Circle verbs and time words. If it's spoken, replay it in your head or ask to hear it again.

2

Highlight the Core

Spot the must-do items: Underline steps, people, tools, and deadlines. Chunk long directions into smaller pieces you can handle.

3

Plan Your Strategy

Pick what helps: Will you rehearse, visualize, write it down, or set a reminder? Say the plan out loud so you know the order.

Ask Yourself Before You Start

  • What is the first thing I need to do?
  • Is there a time, place, or person I can’t forget?
  • Which strategy will help me remember the full direction?
🖍️ Highlight & Chunk
Mark the verbs, underline names and due times, and group long directions into two or three smaller actions.
🔁 Repeat or Rehearse
Say each step out loud once, then quietly rehearse it. Tap or count on fingers if that keeps the order straight.
🎥 Visualize It
Picture yourself moving through each step. Imagine the screen, backpack, or classroom so your brain already knows the path.
✍️ Write or Check Off
Jot the steps in order or draw short boxes to check off. Use sticky notes, a whiteboard, or the notes app on your device.
🗣️ Ask & Clarify
If something is unclear, ask sooner rather than later. Repeat the part you heard and say, “Can you confirm this piece?”
⏰ Set Reminders
Need to remember a time or final step? Set a phone reminder, alarm, or digital calendar alert right away.
Pro Tip: Finish by restating your plan: “First I’ll…, then I will…, and finally I’ll…” Saying it or writing it cements the steps before you take action.
